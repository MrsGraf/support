Skills training: Analysing cartoons

Cartoon / caricature: A cartoon is an amusing or critical drawing in a newspaper or magazine that deals with human nature or a current political or social issue. It often reduces a complex issue to a memorable visual message.

Start by identifying the cartoon and its central idea.

  • What is your first impression of the cartoon?
  • What is the cartoon about? What is its topic or central idea?
  • Is it in black and white or in colour?
  • What is the name of the cartoonist?
  • Where and when was the cartoon published?

Useful phrases for introducing a cartoon

  • The cartoon was drawn by ... and was published / appeared in ... on ...
  • It was created in response to ...
  • At first glance, the cartoon appears to ...
  • It shows / deals with / refers to / comments on ...
  • The cartoonist makes fun of / criticises ... for ...
Keep it concise: Your introduction should provide the basic information and lead towards the topic. Detailed observations belong in the description.

Describe what can be seen before explaining what it means.

  • Which people, objects and details are shown? Where are they positioned?
  • What is happening in the foreground, middle ground and background?
  • Which colours and drawing style does the cartoonist use?
  • Are there any labels, captions or speech bubbles? What do they say?
  • Which people, events or current trends does the cartoon refer to?

Useful phrases for describing a cartoon

  • The cartoon consists of / is made up of ... / is divided into ...
  • The cartoon is in black and white / in colour.
  • The cartoonist uses thick outlines, geometric shapes and vivid colours.
  • In the centre / foreground / background, ...
  • At the top / bottom, ...
  • On the left-hand / right-hand side of the cartoon, ...
  • There is a short / ironic caption underneath. It says / states that ...
  • There is a speech bubble / thought bubble / label in / on / next to ...
  • The speech bubble / label reads “...”
Stay objective: In the description, name visible details systematically. Do not interpret their effect yet.

Explain how the visual and verbal techniques create and communicate the cartoon’s message.

  • symbols
  • irony
  • exaggeration
  • caricature
  • wordplay
  • labels
  • captions
  • speech bubbles
  • colour
  • composition
  • Are figures, groups or issues presented positively or negatively? How is this achieved?
  • Which techniques are used, and what effect do they have?
  • How do drawing style, colour scheme and composition contribute to the overall impression?
  • Does the cartoon criticise particular people, current events or social trends? If so, what is criticised and how?
  • What point is the cartoonist trying to make? How is the message conveyed?

Description

The politician is drawn with an oversized head.

Analysis

The exaggerated head caricatures the politician and makes him appear self-important.

Useful phrases for analysing the message

  • The cartoon represents / is a caricature of ...
  • The cartoon plays on the stereotypical view of ...
  • ... is exaggerated / stressed / symbolic of ...
  • The characteristics of ... are simplified / exaggerated through ...
  • The cartoon is particularly eye-catching because of its use of ...
  • The symbol of ... represents / suggests ...
  • The contrast between ... and ... highlights ...
  • The humour lies in the discrepancy / contrast / parallel / misunderstanding between ... and ...
  • The use of ... reinforces the cartoonist’s message that ...
  • The cartoon conveys its message / gets its message across through ...
Build each point: Name the technique, refer to a precise visual detail, explain its effect and connect it to the overall message.

Assess the cartoon as a visual argument and support your judgement with evidence.

  • How effective is the cartoon as a visual message? Give reasons and use your background knowledge.
  • Is the cartoon’s message convincing? Why or why not?
  • Do you agree or disagree with the cartoonist’s position?
  • If the cartoon is compared with a text: To what extent does it support, complement or contradict the text’s message?

Useful phrases for evaluating a cartoon

  • The cartoon (only partly) achieves its aim of ...
  • By combining ... with ..., the cartoonist succeeds in ...
  • The cartoonist is (not) successful in presenting / criticising ... because ...
  • The cartoon is convincing / simplistic / confusing / unfair because ...
  • The cartoon offers a powerful / oversimplified view of ...
  • The cartoonist skilfully / effectively shows ...
  • Unfortunately, it remains unclear whether ...
  • I agree / disagree with the cartoonist’s position because ...
  • In comparison with the text, the cartoon supports / complements / contradicts the idea that ...
Remember: Evaluation is more than personal preference. Base your judgement on the cartoon’s techniques, message, context and effect on the viewer.
