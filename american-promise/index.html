Exam Training

Use the three parts of a typical written exam to find the support you need. The operator overview shows what each task is asking you to do; the linked pages provide more detailed guidance and examples.

1Comprehension

Show that you have understood the text and can present the relevant information clearly and concisely. Comprehension tasks always test your selective understanding of the text: you will usually need to leave out information that is not relevant. Pay close attention to the focus specified in the task and select only the information that directly answers it.

Operators

These task words show what you are expected to do in this part of the exam.

outlineGive the main features, structure or general principles of something.
summarize / sum upGive a concise account of the main points or ideas.
statePresent the main aspects briefly and clearly.
describeGive a detailed account of what somebody or something is like.

2Analysis

Examine how a text works. Support your findings with suitable evidence and explain how individual aspects contribute to meaning, effect or the overall argument.

Operators

These task words show what you are expected to do in this part of the exam.

analyseDescribe and explain relevant aspects in detail.
examineDescribe and explain a particular aspect in detail.
explainMake something clear by giving reasons, details or relevant aspects.
compareShow relevant similarities and differences.
characterizeProvide a thorough analysis of a character.
interpretExplain the meaning, purpose or message of something.

3Comment / Re-creation of text

Develop your own well-founded response or produce a text for a specific communicative situation, purpose and audience.

Operators

These task words show what you are expected to do in this part of the exam.

comment (on)State your opinion clearly and support it with evidence or reasons.
discussGive reasons for and against an issue and come to a well-founded conclusion.
assess / evaluateExpress a well-founded opinion on the nature or quality of something.
write (+ text type)Produce a text with the specific features of the required text type.

The operator descriptions follow the NRW operator overview for English used from the 2025 Abitur onwards. The exact requirements always depend on the complete task.
