Skills training: Mediation

1

Opening · Connect with your audience

Address your listeners and connect your contribution directly to the communicative situation. Introduce the focus without writing an essay-style introduction.

Referring to the source: If it helps your audience, mention the German source naturally. You do not need a TATTT sentence or line references.
Useful phrases Good morning everyone. · As part of today’s discussion on ..., I would like to focus on ... · To give you an idea of the situation in Germany, I would like to draw on a German article about ... · A German article published by ... shows that ...
2

Main Part · Convey the relevant information

Select and reorganise the information needed for the task. Group related ideas, explain unfamiliar cultural references where necessary and make the progression easy to follow for a listener.

Keep source and own voice apart: Report the source accurately and objectively. Do not add your own knowledge or opinion unless the task explicitly asks you to do so.
Useful phrases First of all, ... · Another important aspect is ... · The article also points out that ... · According to the study referred to in the article, ... · For those of you who may not be familiar with ..., ... · In Germany, ... refers to ...
3

Closing your contribution · Round off the situation

Bring your contribution to a natural and memorable close. Highlight the key takeaway, return to the discussion or thank your listeners. Do not add a new argument or suddenly introduce a personal judgement.

This is not an essay conclusion: The ending should fit the speaking situation rather than mechanically weighing up arguments.
Useful phrases What this shows is that ... · The key point to take away is that ... · So when we discuss ..., we should keep in mind that ... · I hope this has given you an idea of ... · Thank you for listening.
